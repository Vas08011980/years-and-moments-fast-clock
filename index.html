How the simulation was built

  • Daily questions (burst 1). Each person's prompts form a time series: mood depends on the previous prompt's mood (autoregression .30) and on the previous prompt's digital exposure through a person-specific effect drawn from a normal distribution (mean = average daily effect, SD = between-person SD). Lags are used only within the same day. Intraclass correlations of mood and exposure are .40.
  • Missing prompts. Each person's compliance is drawn from a beta distribution (mean = the stated compliance, SD .15), and prompts are missed at random at that rate. Residuals are normal and exposure is generated without measurement error.
  • Analysis. Each simulated burst is analysed with a lagged multilevel model in lme4 (observed person-mean centring, random effect of exposure). The between-person difference is tested with a likelihood-ratio test of the random-slope variance (mixture χ²). Mplus latent-centred DSEM Monte Carlo templates are included to confirm key cells.
  • Bridge question. Four bursts with retention of 100/92/84/75%; person effects correlate .70 across bursts; next-burst wellbeing = .60 × current wellbeing + bridge effect × standardised person effect. Each person's effect is estimated per burst (empirical Bayes) and entered into a multilevel regression of next-burst wellbeing (compliance 67%, average daily effect .10).
  • Sources. Effect sizes and compliance come from published studies (Beyens et al., 2020; Wen et al., 2017). The bridge effect has no direct published anchor, so a range (.10–.30) is simulated. Each cell uses 150 replications; intervals are ±1.96 Monte Carlo standard errors.

Pilot estimates (months 7–12) will replace these design assumptions before the final sample size is fixed.
